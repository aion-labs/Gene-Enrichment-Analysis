## Executive summary

The iGEA network is not distinguishable from what random gene lists produce by the specified test: its largest cluster contained 30 nodes, versus a random mean of 42 (empirical p = 0.63). The list has recognizable pockets of biology, including B-cell regulation (CR2, FCGR2B), intestinal lipid handling (NPC1L1, LDLR), and RNA splicing (RNPC3, SNRNP200). What’s surprising is that some of the overlaps join annotations that are biologically quite different, such as lupus and the initiation of DNA replication. Those overlaps are more useful for understanding why the annotations appear together than for inferring a single biological program.

## Communities

**C1 — Immune regulation, steroid responses, and chromatin.** CR2 helps B cells respond to complement-tagged antigens, while FCGR2B can dampen B-cell activation. IL10 is an anti-inflammatory cytokine that appears in both the lupus and steroid-response annotations. The unexpected overlap with replication initiation comes from shared histones (H2AC14, H2BC14, H3C8), not from evidence that the immune genes control replication.

**C2 — RNA processing.** Several genes point to the machinery that removes introns from RNA. RNPC3 and CENATAC help process the small subset of introns handled by the minor spliceosome, while SNRNP200 helps remodel the major spliceosome.

**C3 — Intestinal lipid handling.** NPC1L1 helps intestinal cells take up cholesterol, while DGAT1 helps package fatty acids into triglycerides. LDLR adds a link to lipoprotein uptake, but its appearance in the broader protein-binding category doesn’t make that category part of intestinal lipid absorption.

**C4 — Muscle differentiation and innate lymphoid cells.** SIX1 fits the muscle side, while EOMES fits the innate lymphoid side through its role in natural killer cell differentiation. EOMES is shared by the two annotations, but that alone is a thin basis for treating them as one developmental program.

**C5 — Cilia and sensory biology.** CEP41 and CLUAP1 fit ciliary biology, while CRX points more specifically to photoreceptor gene regulation. The syndrome label is a clue to those functions, not evidence of a diagnosis.

**C6 — Nitric oxide and vascular signaling.** AGTR2 can promote nitric oxide signaling, and NDNF has been linked to endothelial responses. That gives this group a plausible vascular thread without placing every gene in the same pathway.

**C7 — Restraints on blood-vessel sprouting.** MIR200C, MIR361, and SPRED1 point to ways of limiting endothelial migration. This is biologically adjacent to C6, but the two groups do not share genes here.

**C8 — Limits on muscle growth.** RGS2 and STUB1 point to different ways of tuning growth: regulating receptor signaling and controlling protein turnover, respectively. This is distinct from the differentiation theme in C4: becoming a muscle cell and growing larger are different processes.

**C9 — Endocytosis and protein trafficking.** CCDC32 fits clathrin-dependent uptake, while UNC119 is better known for trafficking lipid-modified proteins. The common theme is protein routing, not necessarily action at the same step.

## Putting it together

The clearest links are local. NPC1L1 and LDLR help explain why the two lipid terms appear together. CR2 is the only cross-community bridge: its complement-related role gives it a clear place in B-cell biology, and its broader binding annotation puts it alongside LDLR. That explains why the immune and lipid-related groups touch without implying that CR2 couples B-cell regulation to intestinal cholesterol uptake. The vascular and muscle themes also have plausible connections within their respective areas, but there’s no compelling reason to weave all of them into one mechanism.

How was the list generated—what assay, cell or tissue, perturbation, and comparison produced it? With that context, we can revisit what fits the experiment, what looks incidental, and what might actually be new.
