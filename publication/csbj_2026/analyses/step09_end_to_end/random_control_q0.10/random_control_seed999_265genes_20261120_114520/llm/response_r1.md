## C1 — Tuning B-cell and complement responses

The mechanistic background here is not supplied by the network, but **FCGR2B** encodes an inhibitory antibody receptor that can restrain B-cell activation when antibody-containing complexes engage it alongside the B-cell receptor. **CR2**, by contrast, recognizes complement-tagged material and can enhance B-cell responses, so its inclusion should not be read as evidence that all these genes act as equivalent immune brakes. **C4BPB** encodes the beta chain of a complement-regulatory complex, providing a connection to extracellular control of immune responses rather than simply another B-cell signaling component. **PARP3**, better known for roles in DNA repair, is the surprising member; the specific evidence underlying its immune annotation would need checking before assigning it a central role in this interpretation.

## Putting it together

The tentative common thread is **feedback between antibodies, complement and B-cell responsiveness**. Antibody complexes can recruit inhibitory signaling through FCGR2B, while complement-tagged antigens can engage CR2 and alter the threshold for activation. Those mechanisms can coexist because immune regulation involves balancing reinforcing and restraining signals—not merely switching immunity off.

However, this result does not establish that those mechanisms are operating together in the source material. There is no basis here for identifying a dominant hub or a gene linking independently supported biological themes, and PARP3 remains an unresolved connection. Given the random-list comparison, proposing an assay, cell type or perturbation would go beyond the evidence.
