## C1 — Antibody feedback and complement-linked regulation

For mechanistic context beyond the supplied annotations, FCGR2B is an inhibitory antibody receptor that can restrain B-cell activation when antibody-containing complexes engage it alongside the B-cell receptor. CR2 instead helps B cells recognize complement-tagged material and can lower their activation threshold, while C4BPB encodes a component of the complement-regulatory C4b-binding protein complex. These genes therefore fit a shared theme of controlling responses to antibody- and complement-marked material, but they do not all imply suppression through the same mechanism. PARP3, better known for DNA damage responses and repair, is the surprising member: its specific connection to this immune annotation deserves checking rather than being treated as evidence for a unified signaling pathway.

## Putting it together

The most useful tentative story is **control of the threshold and feedback of antibody responses**, rather than generalized immune inhibition. Complement tagging can make material more readily recognized by B cells, whereas antibody-dependent inhibitory signaling can help limit further activation (CR2, FCGR2B); regulation of complement provides another layer of control (C4BPB). That functional relationship explains why these biological ideas might appear together without requiring the genes to act in one direction.

There is no basis here for assigning a privileged hub or bridge role to any gene, or for extending this small signal to the whole list. In particular, the enrichment does not establish that B-cell responses were reduced. Given the random-list comparison, it also does not support a defensible guess about the assay, cell type, or perturbation.
