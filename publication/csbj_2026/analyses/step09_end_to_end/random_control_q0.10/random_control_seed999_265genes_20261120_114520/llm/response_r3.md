The limited biological hint is regulation of antibody-related immunity, involving an inhibitory antibody receptor and complement-associated proteins (FCGR2B, CR2, C4BPB). ## C1 — Tuning antibody and complement responses

This is not shown in the network, but established biology provides a possible connection: antibodies and complement can jointly influence how strongly B cells respond to an antigen. FCGR2B supplies an inhibitory feedback mechanism when antibody-containing complexes bring it together with the B-cell antigen receptor, whereas CR2 recognizes complement-tagged material and can help lower the threshold for B-cell activation. C4BPB belongs to the C4b-binding protein complex, linking the theme to extracellular complement regulation, although its membership should not be taken to mean that the beta chain itself performs every regulatory function of that complex. The surprises are that CR2 often supports activation rather than inhibition, and that PARP3 is better known for DNA-repair-related functions; their inclusion does not by itself explain a shared suppressive mechanism.

## Putting it together

The most defensible story is a **possible connection to the balance between immune recognition and restraint**, not evidence that B-cell immunity is suppressed. FCGR2B is the clearest mechanistic anchor for inhibitory feedback, while CR2 and C4BPB bring in complement-associated recognition and regulation. PARP3 remains the least straightforward part of that interpretation and would warrant checking the underlying annotation before assigning it an immune role.

The statistical result does not support inferring an assay, cell type, or perturbation from this signal alone. How was the gene list generated—what assay, perturbation, cell type, and comparison were used? With that context, we can rebuild the interpretation around what fits the experiment, what does not, and what might genuinely be new.
