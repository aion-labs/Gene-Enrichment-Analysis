### C1 — How B cells tune their response to antigen

The receptor mechanisms are not shown in the network, but they explain why the two genes make an interesting pair. When an antibody–antigen complex brings the inhibitory receptor FcγRIIB (FCGR2B) together with the B-cell antigen receptor, it can dampen the signal. By contrast, complement-tagged antigen can engage CR2 alongside the antigen receptor and make the B cell more sensitive to that antigen. The other genes are less direct fits: C4BPB encodes a subunit of a soluble complement regulator, whereas PARP3 is better known for its role in DNA repair.

### Putting it together

The most useful idea to carry forward is that antibodies and complement can change how strongly a B cell responds to the same antigen. The two receptors make that idea concrete, but the link to C4BPB is broader, and PARP3 is the surprise: its DNA-repair role does not provide an obvious link to either receptor mechanism. There isn’t enough evidence here to infer that these genes act together or to favor a particular experimental context.
