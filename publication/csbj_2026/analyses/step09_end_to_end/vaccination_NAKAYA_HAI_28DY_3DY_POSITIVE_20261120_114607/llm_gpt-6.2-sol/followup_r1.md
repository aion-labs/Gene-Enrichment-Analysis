## 1. Revised summary

The network fits an early blood signature associated with a later influenza vaccine antibody response: people with higher day-28 hemagglutination-inhibition (HAI) titers tended to have higher day-3 expression of genes involved in myeloid sensing, particle uptake, and intracellular processing. The network was clearly distinguishable from random gene lists, but that test establishes biological coherence, not predictive performance in a new cohort. My original estimate was partly right about blood and a strong myeloid contribution, but wrong about the comparison: this was variation among vaccine recipients, not a contrast between inflamed and control samples. The most informative feature is that the granule and lysosome program accompanies both phagocytic machinery and inflammatory signaling (TCIRG1, HCK, NCF2, PYCARD). The question now is whether people with higher later titers had more of the relevant cells in their day-3 sample, a different state within those cells, or both.

## 2. What fits

**Well established.** This is not in the network, but human influenza vaccination studies have shown that early changes in blood innate immunity can precede later antibody responses, and that pre-existing immunity strongly influences post-vaccination titers. Day 3 is therefore a reasonable time to look for an early response signature rather than for the machinery making the antibodies measured on day 28. C2 fits that timing: interferon-response and cytokine-receptor genes (IFITM3, IFNAR1, IL10RB) suggest that the cells contributing to the signature are equipped to respond to an inflammatory environment. They do not identify which cytokine was present. In particular, I would not read the cytokine signature as evidence that IL-6 drove the association.

**Plausible, but less directly established.** C1, C3, and C5 fit together as a sequence: actin reshapes the membrane, uptake machinery encloses cargo, and vesicle traffic brings it into contact with acidic compartments and degradative enzymes (ACTR3, SYK, RAB7A, TCIRG1). That makes antigen handling a plausible interpretation, but the genes could just as readily mark the abundance or readiness of circulating phagocytes. Blood neutrophils need not be the cells presenting vaccine antigen to helper T cells for their transcripts to track a productive response elsewhere. Indeed, the clearest result here may be that the early blood profile marks a favorable immune response without identifying the cells that ultimately drive it.

## 3. What does not fit

The extent of the **neutrophil granule and oxidative-burst signature** is more surprising than the presence of innate signaling itself. Uptake of vaccine material is plausible, but a strong representation of granule proteins and oxidase components (SERPINB1, CSF3R, NCF1, NCF2) does not tell us that neutrophils degranulated or generated an oxidative burst after vaccination. A higher granulocyte fraction, baseline differences between recipients, or differences in which cells the blood preparation retained could all produce a similar expression pattern. Likewise, the apparently brain-specific annotations are better explained by phagocyte machinery shared with microglia than by a brain-specific response to vaccination (HCK, SYK, CSF1R).

C6 is the least obvious fit. Its signal rests heavily on three closely related sulfotransferases (SULT1A1, SULT1A3, SULT1A4), so I would check whether the assay could distinguish them before interpreting them as three separate observations. C4 is more credible biologically but less specific: glycosylation, Golgi traffic, and lipid turnover could reflect differences in cell composition or general cell state rather than a vaccine-specific metabolic program. The interesting part of C4 is not the breadth of its metabolism, but the subset that connects back to lysosomal function.

## 4. What may be new

**Phagosome processing as a correlate of antibody response.** The C1 hub TCIRG1 anchors the acidification machinery, while two bridges, ATP6V0D1 and RAB7A, connect that machinery to the C3 uptake and phagocytic-vesicle genes. This is more specific than a generic claim that early inflammation predicts later titers: it suggests that the state of the compartments that receive and process cargo might matter. I’m confident in that functional interpretation of the genes, but only moderately confident that it represents a vaccine-induced change within cells. Nor would faster acidification necessarily help: the conditions that favor cargo destruction are not always the ones that favor antigen presentation.

**Inflammatory sensing coupled to uptake.** The C2 hub CASP1 and the bridge PYCARD make inflammasome machinery a more concrete candidate than a broad cytokine label alone. PYCARD also connects to the granule and uptake programs in C1 and C3. The interesting hypothesis is that people with higher later titers mount a coordinated early response involving both cargo handling and inflammatory sensing. Confidence is lower here: the genes could come from different cell populations, and expression of inflammasome components does not establish their activation.

**Lysosomal lipid turnover as a companion to antigen handling.** GBA1 and M6PR bridge the C1 lysosomal program and C4 metabolism. One points to lysosomal lipid breakdown; the other points to delivery of lysosomal enzymes. Together they make a narrower, more testable candidate than “metabolic rewiring”: do higher responders differ in lysosomal enzyme delivery or function? I’d treat this as exploratory until it can be separated from general differences in myeloid cell abundance or lysosome content.

## 5. Priorities

1. **Separate cell abundance from cell state.** In an independent cohort, collect paired pre-vaccination and day-3 blood, measure absolute neutrophil and monocyte counts, and profile expression in purified populations. Test whether the day-3 signature and the change from baseline each associate with strain-matched day-28 HAI after accounting for pre-vaccination titer. This would distinguish a pre-existing predictor from a vaccine-associated change and show whether either association survives adjustment for cell composition.

2. **Find out whether the trafficking signature corresponds to a functional difference.** In fresh day-3 neutrophils and monocytes, use fluorescent, noninfectious vaccine antigen to measure uptake separately from delivery to acidic compartments. Compare those readouts with the cell-specific expression of the trafficking genes and later HAI. Separating uptake from acidification matters: a stronger signal could mean that more antigen entered the cell, not that the cell processed each particle differently.

3. **Test whether the inflammatory machinery was active.** In paired pre-vaccination and day-3 samples, measure caspase-1 activity and mature IL-1β in the relevant myeloid populations alongside the expression signature. Ask whether these measures add information about day-28 HAI beyond baseline titer, cell counts, and the trafficking signature. If they do, that would justify investigating a distinct inflammatory contribution; if they do not, the inflammasome genes may be more useful as markers of cell state than as an explanation for the antibody response.
