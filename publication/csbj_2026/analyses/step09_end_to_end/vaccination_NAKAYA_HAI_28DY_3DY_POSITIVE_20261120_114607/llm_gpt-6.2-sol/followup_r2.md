## 1. Revised summary

The network fits a day-3 blood signature associated with the later antibody response to seasonal influenza vaccination: its clearest signals are early innate immune sensing, phagocyte activity, and the machinery that handles engulfed material. My earlier estimate was right about the blood expression readout and the strong myeloid contribution, but the key context I missed was that these genes distinguish people with different later antibody titers, not necessarily people with different responses to a perturbation at day 3. The neutrophil-like granule signal (CSF3R, FPR2, SERPINB1) is particularly striking because the outcome is an antibody measurement, yet much of the signature comes from cells that do not make antibodies. The most interesting possibility is that early myeloid state carries information about the later response through cargo handling and inflammatory signaling (RAB7A, ATP6V0D1, PYCARD). The first question, though, is whether higher responders had more of the relevant cells, different states within those cells, or simply higher titers before vaccination.

## 2. What fits

**What is well established.** This is not in the network, but human influenza vaccination can produce early changes in circulating innate immune cells before the later antibody response, and pre-existing immunity strongly influences post-vaccination titers. That makes the cytokine-sensing component of C2 a good fit for the sampling window. Interferon and other cytokine receptors (IFNAR1, IFNGR2, IL10RB) are consistent with cells poised to respond to their surroundings. They are not, on their own, evidence of a specific interferon or IL-6 response: a receptor-rich profile could reflect which cells were in the sample as much as what those cells were doing.

**What is plausible but less established.** The combination of granule and lysosomal machinery in C1, engulfment and oxidant machinery in C3, and actin remodeling in C5 suggests that higher responders differed in some aspect of phagocyte biology. Receptor-proximal kinases and actin regulators (SYK, HCK, PAK1) help explain why uptake and cell movement appear together, while lysosomal enzymes and proton-pump subunits (CTSD, TCIRG1, ATP6V0D1) point to what happens after uptake. That is a coherent early signature, but it is a bigger leap to say that circulating neutrophils handled vaccine antigen or improved its presentation. They could instead be reporting a broader innate state shared with antigen-presenting cells elsewhere. Also, unless the association was adjusted for pre-vaccination titer or tested against paired expression changes, “associated with day-28 titer” should not become “induced by vaccination and predictive of the antibody boost.”

## 3. What does not fit

The strongest surprise is how much of the signal looks granulocytic rather than overtly B-cell related. Day 3 is early enough that the absence of a dominant antibody-secreting-cell signature is not puzzling by itself. What matters more is the blood preparation: in whole blood, differences in neutrophil abundance are an obvious explanation; in a mononuclear-cell preparation, granulocyte carryover or low-density granulocytes would be worth checking. The simultaneous presence of monocyte-associated receptors (CSF1R, CX3CR1) makes it especially risky to assign the whole pattern to one cell type.

C4 only partly fits. Golgi trafficking and glycosylation make sense alongside receptor and lysosome turnover, but the coenzyme A branch (PANK2, PPCDC) does not point clearly to influenza vaccination or antibody production. C6 is more of an outlier: three closely related sulfotransferases account for most of the signal (SULT1A1, SULT1A3, SULT1A4). I would check whether the platform can distinguish those transcripts before treating them as separate observations. If the signal is technically sound, I would still look for a stable donor or cell-composition effect before proposing a vaccine-specific role.

## 4. What may be new

**Intracellular cargo handling may be more informative than uptake alone.** C1 and C3 meet at genes that connect phagocytic compartments to late vesicle maturation and pH regulation (RAB7A, ATP6V0D1), with TCIRG1 anchoring the proton-pump signal. M6PR adds a connection to lysosomal enzyme delivery. The interesting hypothesis is that people with higher later titers differ not just in how much material their phagocytes take up, but in what those cells do with it afterward. I’m confident that the genes support that distinction biologically; I’m much less confident that the relevant cargo was vaccine antigen or that the process contributed to the antibody response.

**Uptake-associated and inflammatory states may coincide in the same cells.** In C2, CASP1 anchors an inflammatory processing signal, while PYCARD connects that machinery to the uptake and granule biology of C3 and C1. That makes PYCARD a useful candidate for asking whether the two capabilities track together within a defined myeloid population. The evidence is weaker for actual inflammasome activation: transcript abundance cannot tell us whether caspase-1 was active or inflammatory cytokines were processed. A cell-resolved association would already be informative, even if the functional assays were negative.

## 5. Priorities

1. **Determine whether the neutrophil signal reflects cell abundance or cell state.** In an independent vaccination cohort, collect paired baseline and day-3 samples, measure leukocyte counts and subsets by flow cytometry, and profile sorted neutrophils and monocytes. Test whether the granule signature still associates with day-28 titer after accounting for cell proportions, baseline titer, and age. The decisive result would be an association within a defined cell type, not just a stronger signal in bulk blood.

2. **Separate cargo uptake from subsequent processing.** In the same paired samples, use standardized noninfectious fluorescent particles to measure uptake and subsequent compartment pH separately in neutrophils and monocytes. Ask whether the C1–C3 signature tracks one readout more strongly than the other and whether either readout adds information about day-28 titer beyond baseline titer and cell counts. This would test the functional distinction without assuming that circulating cells encountered vaccine antigen.

3. **Test whether the inflammatory signal extends beyond RNA abundance.** In fresh baseline and day-3 samples, measure active caspase-1 in defined myeloid subsets and assess mature IL-1β where sample handling permits. Ask whether those readouts track the PYCARD/CASP1 signature and retain an association with later titer after adjustment for baseline immunity. If the RNA association replicates but the functional readouts do not, I would keep it as a marker of early myeloid state rather than call it evidence of inflammasome activation.
