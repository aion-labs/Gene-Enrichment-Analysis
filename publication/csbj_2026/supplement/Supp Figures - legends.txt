Supplementary Figure legends (iGEA, CSBJ revision v7; numbered in order of first citation)

Supplementary Figure 1. Terms selected by ORA and iGEA in each gene-set collection for the HIV dependency factors (265 genes).
Stacked bars: terms found by both methods (blue), by iGEA only (teal) and by ORA only (orange); numbers give
both/iGEA only/ORA only. (A) Both methods at p < 0.01 (ORA 255 terms, iGEA 62). (B) ORA terms with Benjamini-Hochberg
FDR < 0.05 within each collection (87) and iGEA terms that passed the term-level filter (empirical q < 0.10; 56). ORA used
the same gene sets (10-600 genes), background and minimum overlap as iGEA. Term lists: Supplementary Tables 4 and 5.

Supplementary Figure 2. Complete iGEA network of the HIV dependency factors with ten gene-set collections (56 terms with
q < 0.10, 168 genes, seven communities). Numbered squares, terms (key on the right, grouped by community; number of genes in
parentheses); dots and labels, genes; bold labels with black outline, bridge genes, which link terms of different
communities. Communities are shaded and coloured as in the key. Each gene is linked to at most one term per collection.

Supplementary Figure 3. iGEA network of the HIV dependency factors with the three Gene Ontology collections (the network of
Figure 2; 26 terms, 138 genes, six communities), with all gene names. Symbols as in Supplementary Figure 2.

Supplementary Figure 4. Networks of random gene lists compared with the HIV network. (A-C) iGEA networks of three random
lists of 265 genes drawn from the analysis background (260-264 genes after validation) and analyzed with the same ten
collections and settings, without the term-level filter (all terms selected at p < 0.01); colours mark communities. The
network of panel A is the random control list of the Results; LLM answers for it are in Supplementary Table 12.
(D) Largest connected cluster of the HIV network (red line) compared with the largest clusters of the networks of 10,000
random lists of the same size, built after the same term-level filter (q < 0.10); left, number of nodes (genes + terms);
right, edges per gene. Empirical p = (r + 1)/(n + 1). Note the logarithmic y axes.

Supplementary Figure 5. Size and density of random-list networks as a function of list size. Random lists of 50 to 1,000
genes (1,000 lists per size) drawn from the analysis background and analyzed with the ten collections and default settings.
(A) Networks of all terms selected at p < 0.01. (B) Networks after the term-level filter (q < 0.10). Left, number of nodes in
the largest connected cluster; right, edges per gene in that cluster. Boxes, interquartile range with median; whiskers,
1.5 x interquartile range; red, mean. Without the filter, the largest cluster grows with list size (median 7 nodes for 50
genes, 147 for 1,000) while edges per gene stays between 1.2 and 1.5; after the filter, random networks remain small
(median 0-7 nodes) at every size.

Supplementary Figure 6. ORA and iGEA networks of the hypoxia (A, B) and influenza vaccination (C, D) gene lists with ten
gene-set collections. (A) ORA, hypoxia. (B) iGEA, hypoxia. (C) ORA, vaccination. (D) iGEA, vaccination. Squares, terms (size
proportional to the number of genes); circles, genes; black outline, bridge genes. Communities were detected with the same
procedure in all panels and are shaded; each legend lists the communities by their lead term (the term with the most genes)
and number of terms. ORA: terms with FDR < 0.05; iGEA: terms with q < 0.10. Network statistics: Supplementary Table 6.
Described in Supplementary Note 1.
