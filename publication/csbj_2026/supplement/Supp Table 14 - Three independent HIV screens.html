iGEA networks of three independent genome-wide siRNA screens for HIV dependency factors

The hit lists of three genome-wide siRNA screens published in 2008 overlap little at the gene level [Bushman et al. 2009]. Each list was analyzed separately with iGEA (ten collections, default settings, 10,000 random gene lists) and the resulting networks were compared at the level of genes, terms and communities. The same comparison was made with ORA.

6. LLM interpretation of the three networks

Same model (gpt-6.2-sol), prompt (Supplementary Table 3) and protocol as for the other networks; in turn 2 the same sentence described the experiment for all three screens: “an siRNA screen in human cells for host factors required for HIV infection (HIV dependency factors)”. Full answers: Supplementary Table 10 (Brass) and 16B–C (König, Zhou).

Brass

Turn 1 · blind guess of the experiment (3 repeats)
  • My best guess, with modest confidence, is a mammalian cell perturbation screen with a readout sensitive to cell-surface protein abundance or receptor signaling.
  • My best guess is a loss-of-function screen in cultured human cells with a cell-surface or ligand-response readout.
  • My first guess, with modest confidence, is a mammalian cell perturbation screen with a surface-protein, secretion, or receptor-response readout.
Turn 2 · revised summary (repeat 1)

The network makes good sense for an siRNA screen for human genes required for HIV infection: the host machinery for receptor availability, membrane traffic, and transcription is well represented, and the network is clearly distinguishable from those produced by random gene lists. The strongest match is CD4, while the transcription group recovers host factors that are already annotated for HIV transcription.

Turn 2 · what may be new (repeats 1–2)
  • GPI-anchor pathway is the strongest candidate for a coherent but unexplained host phenotype
  • junction between Golgi traffic and surface-protein uptake

Checks: every answer covers every community (7/7); genes named per answer 25–46; genes not in the network: none.

König

Turn 1 · blind guess of the experiment (3 repeats)
  • My leading guess is a pooled loss-of-function screen in proliferating human cells, with fitness or outgrowth as the readout.
  • My first guess, with modest confidence, is a pooled loss-of-function screen in proliferating mammalian cells, with hits selected by growth, survival, or a gene-expression reporter.
  • My leading guess, with modest confidence, is a pooled loss-of-function screen with a growth or survival readout in proliferating cultured cells.
Turn 2 · revised summary (repeat 1)

The network fits an siRNA screen for HIV dependency factors, most clearly in its recovery of nuclear pore proteins and host machinery for transcription and RNA processing. The earlier estimate was partly right: this was a loss-of-function screen in human cells, but the readout was infection, not cellular fitness.

Turn 2 · what may be new (repeats 1–2)
  • host RNA-processing phenotype shared by some pore and spliceosome hits
  • proteasome and cell-cycle hits mark a common cell state that changes how the endpoint behaves
  • the screen is especially sensitive to the handoff between RNA processing and export
  • selective protein turnover and general proteasome capacity contribute differently to the screen phenotype

Checks: every answer covers every community (8/8); genes named per answer 23–59; genes not in the network: XPO1.

Zhou

Turn 1 · blind guess of the experiment (3 repeats)
  • My leading guess is a human-cell functional screen with a virus-related readout, possibly HIV-related.
  • My best guess is a human cell-based functional perturbation screen, followed by validation of hits, with an infection-associated or immune-response readout.
  • My first guess would be a cell-based genetic perturbation screen with a host-response or infection-related readout, possibly with follow-up validation.
Turn 2 · revised summary (repeat 1)

The network is clearly distinguishable from those produced by random gene lists, and the experimental context was correctly anticipated: this was a human-cell functional screen for HIV dependency factors. The recovery of CD4 and CXCR4 is the clearest match, provided the virus used in the screen was CXCR4-tropic.

Turn 2 · what may be new (repeats 1–2)
  • respiratory competence is a distinct contributor to the screen phenotype
  • the Mediator signal reflects something more selective than a general drop in transcription
  • a respiratory state that affects the screen endpoint without overt cell loss
  • a common transcriptional sensitivity behind two apparently different findings

Checks: every answer covers every community (5/5); genes named per answer 20–47; genes not in the network: none.

Notes

  • Gene lists. Brass: the list used throughout the paper. König: Table S3 of König et al. 2008 (295 Entrez Gene IDs; 292 current symbols). Zhou: Table S1 of Zhou et al. 2008, the 390 primary hits minus those not expressed in activated T cells or macrophages (the published 311 host factors; 311 current symbols). Identifiers mapped to current NCBI symbols through Entrez Gene IDs (gene_info, gene_history); input genes outside the background are removed by iGEA.
  • iGEA. Hypergeometric test (upper tail), p < 0.01, overlap ≥ 3, gene sets of 10–600 genes, ≤ 30 iterations, ten collections, 10,000 random gene lists per screen (seed 2026), terms kept at empirical q < 0.10; Leiden communities on the term network (best of 100 seeds).
  • Themes. Community pairs with p ≤ 0.01 were grouped into themes; themes with weaker matches (0.01 < p < 0.05: Brass–Zhou NF-κB/AKT, p 0.027; König–Zhou ubiquitin, p 0.021) are shown because they connect communities already linked to a theme or share hub genes (AKT1, RELA). The Brass Golgi community also matches the König nuclear pore community (Jaccard 0.19, p 0.003) through small-GTPase terms (GTPase binding).
  • Comparison with Bushman et al. 2009. The meta-analysis found shared functional categories by annotating the pooled screens; iGEA recovers the same shared themes (Mediator, proteasome, RNA polymerase and splicing, nuclear pore) from each screen separately, as communities, and keeps screen-specific biology visible. ER/Golgi transport, shared in the meta-analysis, is a community only in the Brass network.
