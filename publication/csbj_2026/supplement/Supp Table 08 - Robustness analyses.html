Supplementary Table 8

Robustness of iGEA results

Three analyses test whether the HIV results depend on choices that a user, or the data, could have made differently: (A) the analysis settings, (B) the composition of the input list and near ties in the iterative selection, and (C) redundancy between gene-set collections. In every analysis the random gene lists were generated and analysed in the same way as the gene list being tested.

A · SettingsThe HIV network stays significant under every setting (p < 0.001); the random control never does (p ≥ 0.46).
B · SubsamplingAll 100 subsamples of 90% of the list give a significant network; cluster genes overlap the full-list cluster with median Jaccard 0.74.
C · Collection redundancy82 of 10,439 gene sets have a near-duplicate in another collection; removing duplicates or any one collection leaves the network significant.

B. Input subsampling and near ties

Selecting a term and removing its genes changes all later iterations, so the result is path dependent. 100 random subsamples of 90% of the HIV list (238 genes; seed 2026) were analysed with the default settings and compared with the full-list analysis. All subsamples share one set of 5,000 random lists of their size (the smallest attainable empirical p is 0.0002).

Of the 56 terms kept in the full list, 18 were kept in at least 80% of the subsamples and 46 in at least half (median recurrence 0.71). Near ties — iterations in which the runner-up term had a p-value within two-fold of the selected term — occurred in 35 of 62 iterations of the full analysis; terms selected in a near tie were kept less often in the subsamples (median recurrence 0.58, versus 0.74 for the other terms). Path dependence therefore mainly affects which term represents a group of genes, not the significance or the gene composition of the network. Terms kept in fewer than half of the subsamples:

Notes

  • Default analysis: one-sided Fisher's exact test (the upper tail of the hypergeometric distribution); p < 0.01 for selection; at least 3 input genes per term; gene sets of 10–600 genes after restriction to the background; at most 30 iterations per collection; the ten collections of Supplementary Table 2.
  • Random gene lists: Part A, 2,000 lists of the input size and 200 per reduced size for the q-value adjustment (seed 2026), generated with the setting of each row; Part B, 5,000 lists; Part C, 1,000 lists. Empirical p = (r + 1)/(n + 1). The main analyses use 10,000 lists.
  • Terms kept: terms with empirical q < 0.10, which form the network. Jaccard: size of the intersection divided by the size of the union.
