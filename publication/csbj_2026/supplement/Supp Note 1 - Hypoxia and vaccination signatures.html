Supplementary Note 1

Hypoxia and influenza vaccination signatures

To test iGEA on a short and a long gene list, we analyzed a hypoxia signature of 75 genes [1] and an influenza vaccination signature of 426 genes [2] with the ten gene-set collections and default settings used for the HIV dependency factors.

Networks and communities

Both iGEA networks were significant (empirical p < 0.0001). For hypoxia, all 32 selected terms passed the term-level filter (q < 0.10) and formed four communities, and each collection contributed a different perspective on the response to low oxygen: Hallmark and the Pathway Interaction Database the HIF-1 program, GO Cellular Component the mitochondrial matrix, KEGG, Reactome and WikiPathways glycolysis, and GO Molecular Function the oxygen-dependent 2-oxoglutarate dioxygenases, which Reactome linked to collagen formation and histone demethylation. For vaccination, 70 of 80 terms passed and formed six communities led by neutrophil degranulation, cytokine signaling and phagocytosis.

As for the HIV list, the two methods diverged as collections were added. With ten collections, the ORA networks had 6.7 (hypoxia) and 10.8 (vaccination) edges per gene and the iGEA networks 2.7 and 2.5, and iGEA modularity remained higher than that of ORA at every step (Supplementary Figure 6; Supplementary Table 6).

LLM interpretation

In the context-blinded first turn, all three answers for the hypoxia network proposed an expression comparison under sustained low oxygen (Supplementary Table 15), and all three for the vaccination network a bulk expression signature from blood or inflamed tissue with a strong myeloid contribution, without inferring vaccination (Supplementary Table 16). When told how the vaccination list was generated, the model noted that its estimate was “wrong about the comparison: this was variation among vaccine recipients, not a contrast between inflamed and control samples”, and asked whether people with higher antibody titers had more myeloid cells in their day-3 blood sample, a different state within those cells, or both.
